The Testimony

Essays on لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ — argued as a proposition, and tested against four kinds of evidence.

Every essay here begins with the Qur'an and then asks a single question of the claim it finds there: does it survive testing? Four witnesses are called in a fixed order — the Qur'anic text, the Sunna with its chains graded, the ancient texts that sit outside the tradition, and the material record of manuscript, inscription, and coin. Where they agree, the case is stated. Where they pull against each other, that is said plainly rather than smoothed over.

On these texts

Qur'anic citations give sūra and āya. Ḥadīth citations give collection and number, and state the grading rather than assuming it. Ancient and non-Islamic sources are cited to an edition. Material evidence — manuscripts, inscriptions, coinage — is cited to its shelfmark or find, with dating method and range given, because a date without a method is not evidence.

Nothing here is offered as a ruling. These are arguments, and an argument is a thing you are meant to be able to break.
